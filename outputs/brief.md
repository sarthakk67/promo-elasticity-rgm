## What this does not support

- -0.571 is a lower bound on the true magnitude; aggregation attenuates it. The promo conclusions do not depend on the elasticity level.
- Incremental share is measured per brand, so a unit switched from a rival counts as incremental. For the retailer it is not. Retailer-level incrementality is materially below the figure quoted.
- The dataset has no cost-of-goods field, so gross margin is assumed. That is why the argument is framed as a break-even threshold rather than a profit forecast.
- The pantry-loading result does not control for households timing an intended stock-up to a promotion. Treat it as an upper bound on forward-buying, not a point estimate.
- The break-even depth calculation holds incremental share fixed as depth falls, which will not hold exactly. It is a first-order bound.
